Let's start with a thin wash and see where it takes us. There is no hurry on
this canvas, and nothing on it is a mistake yet — only a shape that has not
been talked into being something else. Pick up the two-inch brush, load it
lightly, and let the bristles do the deciding.

## Laying in the sky

Work from the top down, criss-cross, barely touching. The trick is to let the
white of the canvas keep breathing through; that glow underneath is what makes
an evening look like an evening instead of a wall. If it goes muddy, it is
telling you that you loved it a little too much. Walk away, come back, lay one
clean stroke over the top.

> Nobody arrives knowing how a hillside sits. You find it by putting one
> down, squinting at it, and putting down a better one.

Now the distant hills. *Barely* darker than the sky, because everything far
away has air stacked in front of it, and air is not free. Tap them in, then
knock the bottoms out with a clean, dry brush so they sit **behind** the land
instead of on top of it.

### Where the trees go

A few decisions, made quickly and without ceremony:

- One tall evergreen, off-centre, because the middle is the least interesting
  place anything can stand.
- A smaller one beside it, close enough to be company.
- A suggestion of a third, far back, more shadow than tree.
- An empty space you are tempted to fill. Leave it. That space is doing work.

Load the fan brush, knock the loose colour out of it against the easel leg,
and start at the top: one tap, then two, then wider as you come down — the branches carry snow the
further out they reach, so they bend, and that bend is the whole illusion.

## Down at the waterline

Reflections fall straight down, never sideways, and they are always a little
quieter than the thing they belong to. Pull the colour down in vertical
strokes, then cut across it once with a clean knife to set the surface. That
single horizontal is the difference between water and a smudge.

Here is the same idea, if you prefer it stated plainly:

```text
sky        → lightest, warmest
hills      → sky + a whisper of dark
trees      → darkest, hard edges
reflection → the tree again, softer, upside down
```

If a tree lands somewhere you did not intend, give it a friend and call it a
grove. Nothing here is precious. The canvas is not going to remember, and
neither is anybody else.
